\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Evaluating LLM-based systems requires distinguishing the quality of the artifacts they produce from the quality of the processes, evidence, interactions, and infrastructure that make those artifacts possible. This survey develops a lifecycle-oriented framework covering input and specification, context and retrieval, output, conversation and interaction, agent processes and trajectories, and operational/system quality. Evaluator reliability is treated as an independent meta-evaluation concern. The framework also separates evaluation targets from methods, protocols, and the strength of evidence supporting each quality claim.

The reviewed examples illustrate why this separation matters. Reference-based and semantic metrics assess different aspects of a response; executable benchmarks measure task success but may not explain intermediate failures; trace-aware methods improve diagnosis without necessarily establishing causality; and automated judges require validation rather than implicit trust. Similarly, evaluating multiple dimensions together does not by itself demonstrate that their relationships have been measured. We therefore distinguish isolated component evaluation, explicit cross-stage assessment, black-box end-to-end measurement, and lifecycle-integrated evaluation.

Our proposed research agenda focuses on reproducible and construct-valid assessment, transparent evaluator calibration, evidence-supported failure attribution, and operationally meaningful quality trade-offs. In particular, a composite quality index should not be interpreted as a reliable end-to-end measure solely because it aggregates many scores: its constructs, weighting rationale, dependencies, uncertainty, and sensitivity to critical failures require justification.

The systematic mapping and its narrative synthesis are subject to a necessary evidentiary boundary. Historical retrieval statistics characterize the candidate archive, not the number of eligible peer-reviewed studies. Final study counts, distributions across quality dimensions and method families, relative frequencies of integration levels, and corpus-wide gap claims will be reported only after publication-status verification, deduplication, full-text eligibility assessment, and human-adjudicated coding. The final version will distinguish confirmed findings from hypotheses generated by representative studies.

The central contribution intended by this survey is thus not another undifferentiated catalogue of benchmarks. It is an auditable framework for asking what is measured, how the measurement is validated, and whether evidence actually connects component quality to the behavior of the complete LLM-based system.
